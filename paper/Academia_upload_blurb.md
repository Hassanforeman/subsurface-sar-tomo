# Academia.edu upload — refutation paper

## Title
No Reproducible Evidence for Deep Subsurface Structures Beneath the Giza Plateau: A Controlled Reproduction of Single-Source SAR Doppler Micro-Motion Tomography

## Summary / description (paste into the "Summary" field)
An independent, controls-first reproduction of the SAR Doppler "micro-motion tomography" method that F. Biondi and C. Malanga used to claim deep structures beneath the Giza plateau. Rebuilding the method from the authors' own papers and lapsed patent, I add the elementary controls the original work omits — a look-order null, an in-data positive control, a surface-leakage test, a depth-of-peak guard, and a sub-aperture-count stability test — and apply it to free X-band spotlight data from two independent sensors (Umbra and Capella), the same data class used in the 2025 work.

Findings, step by step: the patent's "steering matrix" is, by its own wording, a discrete Fourier transform, which returns structured output from any input; the reported depths follow from a physically impossible ~22 kHz investigation frequency and amount to an arbitrary relabelling of the depth axis; a faithful reproduction produces a confident but surface-pinned artifact that matches no known structure; five real sites — including the authors' own Vesuvius and a cross-sensor Capella scene — are statistically indistinguishable from their nulls while in-data positive controls pass; and stacking multiple acquisitions of a known-empty site reinforces the artifact rather than revealing structure, so "agreement across 200+ scans" is a property of the shared operator, not corroboration.

The measurement front-end — using a satellite radar as a remote surface vibrometer — is legitimate and remains useful for surface-deformation monitoring; only the deep-subsurface inference fails reproduction and controls. The paper states explicit falsifiability criteria, and the pipeline, controls, and data are open. It is a critique of method and mathematics, not of intent.
