## 1. New findings in the paper itself

**1.1 The paper says its SAR did not see the muon Big Void.** Discussion: "No trace of the presence of a structure
identified as a Big Void, put in evidence by muonic spectroscopy, was in our hands detected by SAR" **[J✓]**. The Big
Void (ScanPyramids 2017, ≥30 m, above the Grand Gallery) is the best-confirmed hidden void in the pyramid, seen by
three independent muon techniques. The paper instead names a *different* object "Big-void (tag 19)", a 23.5 × 9.4 m
box in its CAD model (Fig. 52), and calls muography "controversial". On the one independently established hidden
feature of that size, the method's own authors report a miss.

**1.2 The North Face Corridor: the part known in 2022 was "found", the part not yet known was wrong.**
- Known before the paper: a corridor-like void behind the north-face chevrons (ScanPyramids 2016–17). The paper cites
  it as the "Small Void" and says its own result "seemed to be confirmed by muon spectroscopy".
- The paper's version (tags 17 + 18, Fig. 23 "Tomographic imaging of the new corridor", Fig. 52): a room of
  **15.372 × 5.742 m** behind the entrance ("Little-void") and a horizontal conduit of **43.932 m** that "seems to end
  at the foot of the Grand Gallery" ("Front corridor … just behind the external V-shaped structure" **[J✓]**).
- What was measured afterwards (Procureur et al., Nat. Commun. 2 Mar 2023; endoscope Feb 2023): one corridor
  **≈9 m long, ≈2 × 2 m**, starting 0.8 m behind the chevrons and ending 9.1 m south. No room, no 44 m conduit.
- So: location = prior knowledge (not a test); length, cross-section and connection = forward predictions, and they
  failed by roughly 5× in length and 3–7× in section. The paper hedges that its sizes "include the thickness of the
  material" and that "the numbers shown after the comma can not be significant", which does not cover a 9 m vs 59 m
  difference. The NFC's 2 m section is also below the paper's own stated resolution (3.71 m, arXiv).
- Fig. 23(b) itself: axes in pixels, a jet map with a line drawing laid over it. No scale.

**1.3 The "known structures" section is overlay by eye.** §7.1 images the King's Chamber, "Zed", Queen's Chamber,
Grand Gallery, Grotto and unfinished room by laying the tomogram over the textbook section drawing ("partially
overlapped"). The subterranean chamber is "detected … although with a weak signal"; a corridor "appears to be abruptly
interrupted" and "it is assumed" to follow a red line the authors drew. The paper states "the only possibility of
mistake lies in the visual interpretation of the data" **[J✓]** and labels one large bright target "a false alarm,
generated by the south-west ascending angle of the pyramid" **[J✓]**, i.e. a bright return from the pyramid's own
edge was recognised as spurious only because nothing was expected there.

**1.4 The metres were measured on the drawing.** §7.17: sizes were "evaluated by using Tape Measuring Wall Area
software … employing as internal standard the pyramid's base length" **[J✓]**. The three-decimal figures (43.932 m)
are screen measurements of the CAD model scaled to 230 m, not radar measurements.

**1.5 The acquisition table contradicts the depth calculation [arXiv only; journal Table 1 is an image].**
Table 1: Stripmap, PRF 2 kHz, PRT 0.23 ms, antenna 6 m, duration 5 s, 7 km/s, 650 km.
- PRT 0.23 ms is a PRF of 4.35 kHz, not 2 kHz.
- Either PRF caps the unambiguous Doppler band at 2–4.35 kHz. The text says the data were "synthesized at 24 kHz" and
  uses a "frequency of investigation set by us equal to 12500 Hz" **[J✓ for both numbers]**. A 12.5 kHz signal cannot
  be represented in a band a few kHz wide (same class of problem as the 100 sps seismographs at Vesuvius).
- A 6 m stripmap antenna gives a synthetic aperture of λR/L ≈ 3.4 km and ≈0.5 s on any one point. The depth formula
  is evaluated with A = 42 000 m ("half the total length of the orbit"); 5 s at 7 km/s is 35 km in total. With the
  stripmap aperture the paper's own formula gives δz = 0.48·650000/(2·3358) ≈ **46 m**, not 3.71 m.
- Third value of the dial: Vesuvius 200 Hz, Khufu 12.5 kHz, patent ≈22 kHz. All "set by us".

**1.6 The "internal ramps" slope equals the pyramid's corner-edge slope [arXiv only].** §7.2: ramps "characterized by
an approximate slope of about 42 degrees, located inside the West and East sides". Khufu's faces slope 51.8°; its four
corner edges (arrises) slope arctan(146.6 / 162.9) = **41.99°**. A coincidence is possible; it is also what you would
get if the "ramps" are the bright edge returns of the pyramid traced through the processing. Untested (see H2).

## 2. What this means for the "validation" claim

1. A validation by the NFC has to explain why the paper's NFC is 44–59 m long with a room and a link to the Grand
   Gallery when the real one is 9 m and blind.
2. It has to explain why the same paper could not see the Big Void and substituted another object under that name.
3. Agreement with features published before the analysis (chambers, Grand Gallery, the 2016–17 void) is not blind.
4. The acquisition parameters in the paper cannot deliver the stated resolution by the paper's own formula.
Grade for prebuttal #16: stays **B until the video is seen**; points 1–2 are the strongest and come from their text.

## 3. Other hypotheses for what the Khufu images show (all UNTESTED)

- **H1 (ours, established for flat ground):** accumulated registration noise + DFT + detrend → surface-pinned band.
  Never tested on the pyramid bodies themselves.
- **H2 Topographic parallax (new, physical).** A scatterer at height h above the focus plane lays over toward the
  radar by h·cot θ along the ground line of sight. Across a synthetic aperture the line of sight rotates by δφ, so the
  layover vector rotates and sub-aperture images shift the scatterer in cross-range by ≈ h·cot θ·δφ. This is
  textbook (it is why tall objects smear in wide-angle spotlight SAR) and it is **proportional to height**. On a
  146 m pyramid in an Umbra spotlight collect (δφ ≈ 0.01–0.06 rad, θ ≈ 30–37°) that is of order metres across the
  aperture: several pixels, far above our 0.2 px floor, zero on the flat plateau. Consequences if true: (a) any
  "micro-motion" map lights up the pyramids and outlines their faces and edges without any vibration; (b) the
  paper's "interferometric fringes" stacked up each face "starting from the bottom" (its Figs 9–14) are height
  contours; (c) edge returns at 42° become "ramps" (1.6); (d) it may bear on our own unexplained Giza residuals
  (scene 02-08 has the steepest look). It is a steady drift, linear in look index, so our degree-2 detrend should
  remove most of it; whether theirs does is undisclosed. In their stripmap mode δφ ≈ 0.005 rad, so the effect is
  smaller (tens of cm) but not zero against 3 m pixels.
- **H3 Sidelobes and edge scattering.** Apex, arrises and the stepped courses are strong, regular scatterers; azimuth
  and range sidelobes draw straight streaks from them. The paper's own "false alarm" is one of these.
- **H4 Overlay-guided reading.** Blobs are matched to a drawing placed by the analyst. Any blob field will "contain"
  the chambers once the drawing is laid on top; 5 tomogram panels are overlays on pre-existing drawings.
- **H5 Real but depth-blind vibration.** Wind and traffic do shake the pyramid at a few Hz (global modes). That is
  surface motion with no depth information and is far below 12.5 kHz; a 0.5 s stripmap dwell cannot resolve it.
- **H6 Aliased frequency label.** With a PRF of 2–4 kHz, "12.5 kHz" is not a measurable frequency in the data; it
  only sets the metre scale (1.5).

## 6. Correction and design-phase findings (10 Nov, later the same day)

**6.1 H2 withdrawn.** While building test K the geometry self-test failed my own expectation. For a straight flight
line the set of points with a given range and range-rate is a circle centred on the flight line; its intersection
with a slant image plane that contains the flight line does not move as the radar moves. Height therefore causes
**no** look-to-look shift in a slant-plane image; only orbit curvature leaves a residual. Computed from the real SICD
orbit polynomials (src/test_k_parallax.py, R/Rdot projection per look, agrees with sarpy to within 1 px): shift per
look step per 100 m of height = **0.005 px (02-07), 0.003 px (02-08), 0.07-0.08 px (03-08)**. All are below the
~0.2 px tracking floor and 15-80x smaller than section 3 claimed. The "several pixels" figure used the ground-plane
layover rotation, which is not what a slant-plane sub-aperture image shows. Consequences (a)-(d) listed under H2 do
not follow. Test K was not pre-registered and no displacement data were looked at.

**6.2 Section 5, first bullet, is wrong.** Our Giza centre crops are not flat ground: the SCP of scenes 02-07 and
02-08 is Khufu and of 03-08 is Khafre, and the crops are centred there. All Giza results in the paper are on the
pyramid bodies. Test G (cemeteries) is the flat-ground one. This removes the stated gap rather than opening one.

**6.3 Synthetic design-phase findings (NOT pre-registered, synthetic data only; verify before use).**
- *Shared-band blindness.* Speckle given a common slow-time position drift x(f) = beta*f (the signature of real
  target motion during the collect), looks at 80% overlap: measured shift / expected shift = **0.00** for phase
  correlation and for NCC, at every beta tried (inter-look match 0.56). Reason: adjacent overlapped looks are
  correlated only through the spectral band they share, which is the same data in both, so the correlation peak sits
  at zero lag. With non-overlapping looks speckle decorrelates entirely (already measured, Q3). Point-like targets:
  NCC recovers 0.8 of the drift at 80% overlap, phase correlation 0.04. Reading: on distributed (speckle) ground the
  sub-aperture tracking front end cannot register real slow-time motion in either regime; only isolated strong
  scatterers can. Consistent with the measured adjacent-look match of ~0.6 = filter-bank overlap (Q3).
- *Possible weakness in OUR tracker (open).* On Hann-band-limited synthetic speckle the baseline phase-correlation
  estimator returns about 0 for a true rigid shift once looks are narrow: a true 2.0 px reads 2.04 at the n_sub = 11
  band, **0.11 at n_sub = 21, 0.05 at 32, 0.00 at 64-128** (overlap 0.8 bands; NCC degrades more slowly: 1.77, 1.54,
  0.48, 0.15). A true 0.5 px reads 0.13 even at n_sub = 11. Two independent speckle fields give 0 +/- 0.04 px instead
  of a random shift. If this holds on the pipeline's own code path, then (i) our runs at n_sub >= 21 could not have
  seen real displacement, and "the verdict does not change from 11 to 128 looks" is weaker evidence than stated;
  (ii) the planted controls should be re-checked per n_sub. The n_sub = 11 results are not affected by (i).
  **Verify first, on the repo functions, before the PCI response.**
