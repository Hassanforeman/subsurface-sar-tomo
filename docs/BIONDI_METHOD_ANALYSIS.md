# How Does Biondi Get Depth? — Method Analysis + Recreation/Improvement Plan

*Working analysis built from the five papers Hassan flagged plus the surrounding literature.
Premise we take seriously: Biondi is not fabricating the tomograms — he is running a real
processing chain. The question is which links in that chain are sound, which are unproven, and
what we'd have to add to recreate (and then improve on) his results.*

---

## 1. The five papers, and what each one tells us

1. **Biondi & Malanga (2022), "SAR Doppler Tomography Reveals … the Great Pyramid of Giza"**
   (Remote Sensing 14(20):5231; arXiv:2208.00811; COSMO-SkyMed). The depth paper. Its own
   abstract states the principle plainly: EM waves do **not** penetrate solids, so the imaging
   is built "on the analysis of micro-movements … generated by background seismic waves," and the
   pyramid "becomes transparent like a crystal when observed in the micro-movement domain." This
   is the crucial admission: **the radar never goes underground. The surface vibration is the
   data; depth is inferred from it.**

2. **Biondi, Addabbo, Orlando, Clemente (2019), "Micro-Motion Estimation of Maritime Targets
   Using Pixel Tracking in COSMO-SkyMed SAR"** + the companion **"COSMO-SkyMed Staring Spotlight
   … Micro-Motion and Inclination Angle Estimation of Ships by Pixel Tracking and Convex
   Optimization"** (RemS 11/14/1637 and 11/7/766). These are the **measurement front-end** — the
   method we already partly reproduce. Mechanics: split the raw data of a single **staring
   spotlight** scene into two (or more) partially overlapping, re-synthesized sub-apertures;
   measure sub-pixel displacement between them by normalized cross-correlation (window 128 px,
   oversampling 64); clean the displacement field with a **low-rank + sparse decomposition (LRSD)**
   solved by convex optimization; use a localized Radon transform for orientation. This is solid,
   peer-reviewed surface vibrometry.

3. **Rollo, Ilioudis & Clemente (2025), "Advances in Micro-Doppler Processing in SAR: A review"**
   (IEEE GRSM; University of Strathclyde — Biondi's former group, with overlapping co-authors).
   The authoritative survey of the whole field. Tellingly, it frames micro-Doppler SAR as
   **surface** applications — target vibrometry, ships, **structural health monitoring of bridges
   and dams** (it cites Biondi's bridge and Mosul Dam work) — and lists the field's biggest **gaps
   as the absence of open benchmark datasets and the lack of real-world validation.** It does
   **not** present deep subsurface tomography or the pyramid result as established state of the
   art. In other words, Biondi's own research community treats this as surface vibration sensing;
   the deep-tomography leap is his alone.

4. **Morley & Mather (2024), "Detecting shallow subsurface anomalies with airborne and spaceborne
   remote sensing: A review"** (Science of Remote Sensing). Reviews 70+ techniques (GPR, thermal
   IR, magnetics, InSAR subsidence, hyperspectral). Useful as the **"what actually works shallow"
   benchmark** and as a source of independent cross-validation methods. Confirms that credible
   subsurface remote sensing is overwhelmingly **shallow** and usually multi-sensor.

**Net:** four of five papers describe a legitimate, peer-reviewed **surface micro-motion**
capability. Exactly one (the pyramid paper) makes the depth leap, and it does so by an
inferential inversion step, not by any new penetrating measurement.

## 2. The actual depth-extraction chain (steelman)

Reconstructed as charitably as possible, Biondi's logic is a four-step chain:

1. **Illumination.** Ambient/background seismic energy continuously shakes the ground and any
   structure on it. This is real and ever-present (ocean microseisms, wind, cultural noise).
2. **Encoding.** Subsurface heterogeneity — cavities, layer boundaries, density contrasts —
   reflects, scatters and resonates that seismic wavefield. So the **surface displacement field
   carries an imprint of what lies beneath** it. This is also real: it is the basis of microtremor
   / HVSR site surveys and ambient-noise seismic interferometry.
3. **Measurement.** A single staring-spotlight SAR pass, via Doppler sub-aperture decomposition +
   sub-pixel tracking, measures that surface micro-displacement field to sub-millimetre precision.
   This is his validated ship/bridge method (papers #2).
4. **Inversion.** A steering matrix `A(z)` maps a hypothesised scatterer/resonator at depth `z`
   to the phase (and, via **multichromatic analysis**, the range-frequency) signature it imprints
   across the sub-apertures. Solving `Y = A(z) h(z)` yields "structure vs depth," with
   δz = λ_seismic · R / (2A).

Steps 1–3 are individually defensible. **The entire dispute lives in step 4** — and in whether
steps 1–2 actually survive the brutal constraint that a single SAR pass samples only ~2–3 s of
surface motion, as a handful of sub-aperture looks.

---

## 3. Where it is solid vs. where it breaks

**Solid:** the surface micro-motion measurement (community-validated), and the *existence* of
subsurface information in surface ambient vibration (seismology proves this for **shallow**
structure via resonance).

**Where it breaks (our read, consistent with our four nulls):**

- **Temporal aperture.** Ambient-noise methods in seismology normally need seconds-to-minutes of
  *continuous* recording and many station pairs to build coherent Green's functions. One SAR pass
  gives a ~2–3 s dwell sliced into a few looks — a tiny temporal/Doppler aperture. Deep structure
  simply may not be resolvable from that, regardless of processing.
- **The steering matrix is undisclosed** (HarmonicSAR) and, where independent reproductions
  (not-JASH) reconstructed it, came out **dimensionally inconsistent** and **Nyquist-violating** —
  meaning the claimed fine depth resolution can be aliased artefact.
- **No controls in the original.** No null test, no in-data positive control, no surface-leakage
  check, no ground truth. Our pipeline adds all four and finds the real tomograms indistinguishable
  from shuffled nulls even at a known shallow void (Butte) with a passing positive control.

So the most likely honest description: **Biondi measures a real surface vibration field and then
over-interprets it through an unvalidated inversion that manufactures confident deep structure
from an aperture that cannot support it.** Not fraud — over-reach at step 4.

## 4. What we are missing relative to Biondi (the recreation gaps)

If we want to *recreate* his result rather than just bound it, these are the concrete differences
between his pipeline and ours, in priority order:

1. **Data class / dwell.** He uses **COSMO-SkyMed staring spotlight** — the antenna *stares*, giving
   a very long dwell, a wide Doppler band, hence a larger synthesis aperture `A` (finer δz) **and**
   a longer surface-motion time series. Our free Umbra spotlight has shorter dwell. This is the
   single biggest input difference and a prime suspect for non-reproduction.
2. **Multichromatic analysis (MCA).** He adds **range sub-band** frequency diversity (from his
   DInSAR atmospheric-phase-screen work). We currently use Doppler sub-apertures only. MCA is a
   whole diversity dimension we don't exploit.
3. **Low-rank + sparse decomposition (LRSD).** His signature denoising step — separates coherent
   vibration (low-rank) from noise (sparse) via convex optimization. We detrend and quality-weight
   but don't do LRSD. This could materially clean the micro-motion field.
4. **The steering matrix itself.** Undisclosed. Our inverter is our own (Bartlett/Capon/MUSIC). A
   mismatch here could fully explain divergent results — and is the least recoverable gap.
5. **Sub-aperture count / overlap / oversampling.** He runs 128-px windows at 64× oversampling;
   worth matching or exceeding.

## 5. Recreation plan (phased, cheapest first)

**Phase A — close the free gaps (no spend).**
- Prefer the **longest-dwell** Umbra/Capella **staring-spotlight** scenes we can find; read from
  **CPHD** (full phase history), not SICD, to maximise sub-aperture flexibility.
- Add **LRSD** denoising to `micromotion.py`; raise sub-aperture count, overlap and oversampling.
- Add a **multichromatic (range sub-band)** stage and feed both diversity axes to the inverter.
- Re-run Butte + Vesuvius with the controls. Success criterion is unchanged: above-null,
  leakage-clean, ground-truth-aligned.

**Phase B — the physically defensible "depth": resonance, not tomography.**
- For each patch, compute the **surface micro-motion power spectrum** and test for **HVSR-style
  resonance peaks** (impedance contrast at depth H gives f ≈ v/4H). This is the established,
  publishable form of "reading depth from surface vibration," and it is **directly testable at
  Butte** against the documented shallow level depths (see `docs/BUTTE_GROUND_TRUTH.md`). Even a
  partial hit here would be a real, honest result — and a much stronger commercial story than the
  full tomography.

**Phase C — the decisive paid match (when ready to spend).**
- Task **COSMO-SkyMed staring spotlight** (his exact instrument/mode) over a ground-truthed shallow
  target (Derinkuyu/Cappadocia). This is the only way to truly reproduce his data class; if it is
  still null with our controls, the method is bounded regardless of sensor.

## 6. How we'd try to *improve* on him

- **Enlarge the angular aperture** — the dominant resolution lever: multi-pass stacks, or
  airborne/drone collection (low R, wide arc). His own lapsed patent explicitly allows airborne.
- **Super-resolution inversion** — Capon/MUSIC/compressed-sensing (we already have these) layered
  on the MCA + Doppler diversity.
- **Velocity calibration** — convert the relative depth axis to metres with a site v(z), so results
  are checkable against boreholes/known depths rather than asserted.
- **Multi-sensor cross-validation** — from the Morley & Mather toolbox (GPR, thermal IR, InSAR
  subsidence) over the same footprint, so any SAR "depth" claim is corroborated independently.
- **Honesty as the moat** — open controls, open benchmark data (the exact gap the Strathclyde
  review names), and resonance-based shallow characterization rather than unfalsifiable deep
  imaging.

---

## 7. One-paragraph answer to "how does he get data from deeper underground?"

He doesn't, directly — nothing penetrates. He measures the **surface's** sub-millimetre vibration
(a real, peer-reviewed capability) and then **infers** depth by assuming that subsurface structure
has imprinted itself on that vibration field and inverting for it through an undisclosed steering
matrix. The inference is physically motivated (subsurface heterogeneity really does modulate
ambient surface vibration — that's what microtremor/HVSR surveys exploit) but, from a single pass,
the temporal/Doppler aperture is almost certainly too small to support the deep, high-resolution
3-D reconstruction he reports — which is exactly why his result doesn't survive the controls our
pipeline adds. The credible, recreatable core is **shallow, resonance-based** surface-vibration
sensing; the deep tomography is the unproven leap.

## 8. Disclosed dataset clues — he is NOT working single-pass (verified)

Hassan's hypothesis (the compute/time cost implies a large dataset, not one pass) checks out
against primary sources:

- **His own 2022 abstract** says the results come from "processing **series of SAR images**" from
  COSMO-SkyMed — plural, not a single pass. The "single-pass" framing is something the discourse
  (and our own reproduction) attached; he never claimed it in print.
- **Joe Rogan Experience (Jan 23 2026):** asked "How many different scans have been done on this
  area?" Biondi answers **"More than 200,"** "all with uniform results," and explains they started
  on COSMO-SkyMed then added Capella Space and other satellites for "diversity… confirmation."
- **Pilicy's sympathetic appraisal (Graham Hancock site, 2025):** describes "compiling a dataset of
  precise micro-movements **over time and space**" and confirms the **steering matrix, depth
  parameters, and extent of AI use were never disclosed**. It also points to a **~1.5-hour technical
  presentation by Biondi** (TheProjectUnity/status/1935100234243203529) as the fullest method talk —
  a priority source still to mine.

**What this changes for us:**
1. **Scope correction (important, honest).** Our null result bounds **single-pass free X-band**. It
   does NOT bound Biondi's actual configuration, which is a **large multi-acquisition, multi-sensor
   stack (200+ scenes)**. Our preprint should say this explicitly — it sharpens our claim rather than
   weakening it ("single pass is insufficient; the stacked version is untested here").
2. **The missing lever is multi-acquisition stacking** — see `VALIDATION_PLAN_v2.md` §Lever 0. A stack
   buys two things a single pass cannot: a real **multi-baseline/angular aperture** (directly fixes
   our conditioning critique → finer δz) and a long **temporal record** of the surface vibration
   field (the seconds-to-months that ambient-noise seismology actually needs).
3. **Double-edge (keep honest).** "Uniform results across 200 scans" is weaker evidence than it
   sounds: a *processing-induced* artifact (e.g. the off-grid alias we demonstrated in
   `steering_stress_test.py`, or a fixed steering-matrix bias) would also reproduce uniformly across
   every scene. Consistency proves the artifact is systematic, not that it is real. The deciding test
   remains a known-ground-truth shallow site, now run in **stacked** mode.
